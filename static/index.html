What this application evaluates

This local prototype implements the paired documentation-time framework in Proof of Concept.docx. Its five example clinicians are simulated. CSV uploads can replace those pairs with de-identified research data. It does not implement an ambient scribe, a live Epic integration, or a clinical decision support service.

Analysis specification

The unit of analysis is the clinician. Pre/post values should be clinician-level mean active documentation minutes per encounter over prespecified comparable periods. Define active time, idle-time thresholds, encounter inclusion criteria, and aggregation rules before exporting EHR data. Raw EHR timestamps alone do not measure active documentation time.

Differences are pre minus post. Relative reduction is 100 × (mean pre − mean post) / mean pre. The paired t-test is two-sided, with n − 1 degrees of freedom and a 95% Student t confidence interval for the mean difference. Cohen’s dz is mean paired difference divided by the sample standard deviation of differences. A zero baseline makes relative reduction undefined; zero variance makes inference undefined.

Clinicians must be independent and differences approximately normally distributed. Inspect outliers and distributional assumptions, especially with five pairs. No adjustments for confounding, multiplicity, missing data, or clustered encounters are implemented. Do not treat multiple encounters from the same clinician as independent rows.

Workload is not burnout

Workload imports require consistently scored 0–100 NASA-TLX values. Record whether you use weighted TLX or the unweighted mean of the six subscales (Raw TLX). This app analyzes aggregate scores; it does not administer the instrument. The document’s simulated baseline of 461.3 is not a standard 0–100 score and is intentionally not reproduced as validated NASA-TLX data. Workload scores do not establish burnout or patient engagement outcomes.

Simulation specification

CDS counts use NumPy default_rng(seed) with two independent binomial draws. The default input probabilities are 89% and 34%, with 10,000 alerts per group. Wilson 95% intervals summarize each proportion. This differs from the document’s legacy random-number sequence and is reproducible with the pinned package versions. Rates are descriptive; lower overrides alone do not establish clinical benefit.

Synchronized publication outputs

The original five-pair example, the separate 5,000-row demonstration, and the CDS count model are rebuilt into the proof-of-concept document and suggested Results text by publication/build.py. The simulation results page includes a separate 24-scenario normal-difference experiment with Monte Carlo uncertainty. It is numerical validation under stated assumptions, not clinical validation. No measured burnout or workload results are included.

Local data handling

Data is processed in memory by a localhost Python server. The app has no telemetry, external assets, database, or automatic persistence. Downloads are saved by your browser. This prototype has no authentication or institutional deployment controls; use de-identified data.
