## Suggested wording, pending verification of the workflow and CDS sources

### A. Documentation and workflow time

The supplied summary values indicate that documentation time decreased from 16.0 to 4.5 minutes per encounter, a 71.9% relative reduction. Using the category assignments in the paragraph, order-entry time decreased from 4.0 to 1.5 minutes and chart-review time from 5.0 to 3.0 minutes. Summing these tasks gives a decrease from 25.0 to 9.0 minutes, or 64.0%, assuming the tasks are nonoverlapping and were measured in comparable encounters. These are descriptive calculations from supplied aggregate values; sample sizes and uncertainty were not available.

Separately, a published pediatric digital-scribe study reported burnout in 28 of 51 paired respondents (54.9%) before implementation and 17 of 51 (33.3%) afterward. This association should be attributed to that study and should not be interpreted as proof that the workflow-time changes shown here caused a reduction in burnout.

### B. CDS usage trends

The supplied six-month series shows alert override rates decreasing from 85.0% in month 1 to 25.0% in month 6, a 60.0 percentage-point decrease. Proactive interaction rates increased from 2.6% to 6.0%, a 3.4 percentage-point increase. These descriptive trends require confirmation against the original telemetry or cited study, including monthly denominators and consistent event definitions. They do not by themselves establish improved diagnostic accuracy, medication safety, or clinical benefit.
